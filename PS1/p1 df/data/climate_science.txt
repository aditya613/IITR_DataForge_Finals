# CLIMATE SCIENCE REFERENCE DOCUMENT
# Authoritative Source for Climate Fact Verification

## Section 1: Global Temperature Trends

The global average temperature has increased by approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius since the pre-industrial era. This warming is primarily due to human activities, particularly the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) confirms that human influence has warmed the climate at a rate unprecedented in at least the last 2000 years.

## Section 2: Carbon Dioxide Levels

Atmospheric CO2 concentration has risen from 280 ppm in pre-industrial times to approximately 420 ppm as of 2024. This represents a 50% increase in atmospheric carbon dioxide.

The current rate of CO2 increase is approximately 2.5 ppm per year. Carbon dioxide levels are now higher than at any point in the past 800,000 years.

## Section 3: Sea Level Rise

Global mean sea level has risen by approximately 21-24 centimeters since 1880. The rate of sea level rise has accelerated significantly in recent decades.

The current rate of rise is 3.7 mm per year. This acceleration is due to thermal expansion of ocean water and melting of land ice.

## Section 4: Arctic Ice

Arctic sea ice extent has declined by approximately 13% per decade since 1979. This decline is one of the most dramatic indicators of climate change.

Scientists project that the Arctic could experience ice-free summers by 2050 under current trends. The Arctic is warming approximately twice as fast as the global average.

## Section 5: Greenhouse Gases

The main greenhouse gases are carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O), and fluorinated gases. Each has different warming potential and atmospheric lifetime.

Methane has 80 times the warming potential of CO2 over a 20-year period. Although methane has a shorter atmospheric lifetime than CO2, its immediate warming impact is much stronger.

Nitrous oxide has approximately 300 times the warming potential of CO2 over 100 years.

## Section 6: Renewable Energy

Solar and wind energy now account for approximately 12% of global electricity generation. This share has grown rapidly over the past decade.

The cost of solar photovoltaic panels has dropped by 99% since 1976. Solar is now one of the cheapest forms of electricity generation in many regions.

Wind energy capacity has grown by approximately 12% annually over the past decade. Offshore wind technology is advancing rapidly.

## Section 7: Climate Impacts

Climate change is causing more frequent and intense extreme weather events. Heat waves, droughts, and heavy rainfall events are becoming more common.

Ocean acidification has increased by approximately 30% since the industrial revolution due to absorption of atmospheric CO2.

Approximately 1 million species are at risk of extinction due to climate change and related pressures.
